Arrange the dashboard

The dashboard is yours to arrange. Four operations, named the same way wherever you meet them: Move a card, Compact it, Resize it or Hide it. Every choice is remembered by the browser you made it in, so each device keeps its own arrangement, and Reset layout in Settings → Interface puts every card, size and column back the way it shipped.

Customise layout is the guided way in, and the quickest way to see what a card can do. Open it from the dim link just above the first card, or from Settings → Interface, where it is the first button in the Dashboard layout row. While it is on, every card you can rearrange is outlined, the three tools in each card's title row are drawn at full strength with their word beside the icon, and every resize bar is drawn in the accent colour; a card that has no bar says why in a line of its own instead of leaving you hunting for one. Done or the Escape key leaves. The mode is a way of looking at the page rather than a setting: it changes nothing about your layout, and it is off again after a reload.

On a dashboard nobody has arranged yet, one line above the cards says they can be moved, resized, compacted or hidden, and points at that link. It is shown once: Got it dismisses it, and so does opening the mode or arranging anything at all.

  • Move. Drag a card by its title. The whole heading is the handle, not only the small icon at its left, so there is no fiddly target to find. That icon is a tab stop as well: focus it and the up and down arrow keys move the card.
  • Compact. The button beside the handle tightens that card's spacing: same figures, less room. On History it switches between the ten most recent downloads and the rest of your history. Compact mode in Settings → Interface does the whole dashboard at once.
  • Resize. A card that holds a list or a chart carries a bar along its bottom edge: drag it to the height you want. The bar is faint until you point at the card, when it draws as a full-width hairline with ridges at its trailing corner; it takes keyboard focus too, where the up and down arrow keys size the card and Shift makes a bigger step. Which cards have one changes as cards gain and lose lists of their own, so take the bar as the answer rather than any list here. In Customise layout the cards without one say so themselves.
  • Hide. The last button in the title row hides that card. Hidden cards collect in a Hidden bar at the foot of the dashboard; click a name there to bring one back.

Right-click a card's title for the same operations in one list: Move up, Move down, Compact, Resize and Hide, plus a way into Customise layout when you are not already in it. Resize is offered only on a card that has a bar to drag. Move up and Move down grey out at the ends, and they count only the cards you can see: a hidden card is stepped over, so first and last mean first and last visible.

That menu is a keyboard path as well as a mouse one. Tab to a card's move handle, press the Context Menu key or Shift+F10, and it opens under the heading. The up and down arrow keys walk it and step over anything greyed out; Escape closes it and puts the focus back on the handle you opened it from. Choosing Resize hands the focus to that card's bar, where the up and down arrows size the card and Shift takes a bigger step.

Escape only ever dismisses the topmost thing. Closing the card menu while the mode is on leaves the mode running; a second Escape leaves the mode.

Dashboard cards in Settings → Interface is the same arranging as a plain list of controls: a Show box for each card, a Compact box beside it, and Move up and Move down buttons. On a phone, a tablet or any narrow window the card tools and dragging are switched off, because dragging a card around a page that scrolls under your finger is a poor control, so this list is how the dashboard is arranged there. It writes what the desktop reads, so an order set on a phone is the order the desktop shows. Its buttons follow the menu's rule: they grey out at the ends of the visible cards, and a card you have hidden cannot be moved until you show it again.

Table columns are arranged in place rather than from any of the above. In the queue, history and browse tables, drag a column heading sideways to move that column, or drag the heading's trailing edge to resize it; double-clicking that edge puts the widths back. With a heading focused, the left and right arrow keys move it, and Shift with an arrow resizes it.
